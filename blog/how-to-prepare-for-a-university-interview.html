How to Prepare for a University Admission Interview

By EduPath Team · Nov 8, 2026 · 8 min read

Many universities and scholarship committees invite shortlisted applicants to an interview. The interview is not a trap. It is a chance for the panel to meet the person behind the application and to check that you are genuinely interested in the programme. With good preparation, you can walk in calm and leave a strong impression.

Step 1: Know Your Own Application

Read your statement of purpose, CV and any essays again. Interviewers often ask about something you wrote, such as a project or a goal. Be ready to talk about every point in more detail. If you need help with your statement, see our guide How to Write a Strong Statement of Purpose.

Step 2: Research the Programme and University

  • Read the course structure, core modules and electives.
  • Look at two or three faculty members whose work interests you.
  • Note the facilities, labs, clubs or exchange programmes you would use.
  • Find out what graduates of the programme go on to do.

Specific details show the panel that you chose this programme for real reasons.

Step 3: Practise Common Questions

  • Tell us about yourself.
  • Why do you want to study this subject?
  • Why this university?
  • What are your strengths and weaknesses?
  • Describe a challenge you faced and how you handled it.
  • Where do you see yourself in five years?
  • Do you have any questions for us?

Do not memorise answers word for word, because they will sound robotic. Instead, prepare two or three key points for each question and practise saying them aloud.

Step 4: Use the STAR Method for Experience Questions

For questions about past experiences, structure your answer like this:

  • Situation: briefly set the scene.
  • Task: explain what you needed to do.
  • Action: describe the steps you took.
  • Result: share the outcome and what you learned.

This keeps your answers clear, specific and around one to two minutes long.

Step 5: Do a Mock Interview

Ask a teacher, friend or family member to interview you, or record yourself on your phone. Watch for speaking too fast, filler words like "um" and answers that wander. Two or three practice rounds make a big difference.

Step 6: Prepare for Online Interviews

  • Test your internet, camera and microphone a day before.
  • Sit in a quiet, well-lit place with a plain background.
  • Look at the camera when you speak, not only at the screen.
  • Keep a copy of your application and a notepad nearby.

Step 7: On the Day

Dress neatly, arrive (or log in) 10 minutes early and greet the panel politely. Listen to each question fully, and it is fine to take a few seconds to think. If you do not understand a question, ask them to repeat it. Honesty matters more than a perfect answer.

Step 8: Ask Good Questions and Follow Up

Prepare one or two thoughtful questions, for example about research opportunities or how students are supported in their first year. After the interview, a short thank-you email is a polite way to finish.

Conclusion

A successful interview comes from knowing yourself, knowing the programme and practising aloud. Prepare key points, use the STAR method and stay honest. The panel wants to see your curiosity and motivation, not a rehearsed speech.
