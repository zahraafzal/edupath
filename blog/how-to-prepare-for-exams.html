Exam Prep

How to Prepare for Exams: A 4-Week Plan

By EduPath Team · Nov 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Exams feel stressful when everything is left until the last few days. With four weeks and a simple plan, you can cover your syllabus, practise properly and walk into the exam hall calm. This plan uses methods that research supports, such as testing yourself and spreading your revision over time. You can adjust it for school, college or university exams.

Before You Start: Make a Topic List

Write down every topic in each subject. Mark each one with a colour: green (I know it well), yellow (I partly know it) or red (I don't know it). This list becomes your map. Spend most of your time on red and yellow topics, not on the ones you already find easy.

Week 1: Understand and Organise

  • Collect your notes, textbook chapters and past papers in one place.
  • Make a weekly timetable with fixed study blocks, for example two or three 45-minute sessions a day.
  • Start with red topics. Read, make short notes and ask your teacher about anything unclear.
  • At the end of each session, close your notes and write down what you remember.

Week 2: Practise and Test Yourself

  • Turn your notes into questions or flashcards.
  • Test yourself instead of re-reading. Research on the "testing effect" shows this helps you remember far more.
  • Revisit Week 1 topics for a few minutes before starting new ones. This spaced review keeps them fresh.
  • Move topics from red to yellow, and yellow to green, as you improve.

Week 3: Past Papers Under Time

  • Solve full past papers with a timer, in exam conditions.
  • Mark your answers using the official marking scheme if it is available.
  • Write down every mistake in a "mistakes notebook" and the correct method next to it.
  • Mix questions from different chapters, so you practise choosing the right method.

Week 4: Review and Rest

  • Go through your mistakes notebook and flashcards every day.
  • Do one or two final timed papers early in the week, not the night before.
  • Prepare your exam materials: admit card, pens, calculator and ID.
  • Keep a regular sleep routine. Sleep helps the brain store what you learned.

The Day Before the Exam

Do a light review of key points, not new topics. Check the exam time and location, pack your bag, eat a proper meal and go to bed at a normal time. Staying up all night usually lowers performance.

Managing Exam Stress

Some nervousness is normal and can even help you focus. If worry feels too strong, try writing down your thoughts about the exam for ten minutes beforehand. A study by Gerardo Ramirez and Sian Beilock (2011) found that students who wrote about their worries before a test performed better than those who did not. Slow breathing, a short walk and talking to someone you trust can also help.

Quick Tips in the Exam Hall

  • Read all instructions and questions carefully first.
  • Start with questions you are confident about.
  • Watch the clock and divide your time by marks.
  • Leave a few minutes at the end to check your answers.

Conclusion

Good exam results come from steady work, not last-minute panic. Organise in week one, test yourself in week two, practise past papers in week three and review and rest in week four. For more on study methods, read our guide How to Study Effectively: 7 Evidence-Based Techniques.

References

  • Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning. Psychological Science, 17(3), 249–255.
  • Ramirez, G., & Beilock, S. L. (2011). Writing about testing worries boosts exam performance in the classroom. Science, 331(6014), 211–213.
  • Dunlosky, J., et al. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58.
